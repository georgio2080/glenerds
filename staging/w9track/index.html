The 1099 threshold rule

In general, if you pay a contractor at or above the federal reporting threshold in a calendar year for services, you must file a Form 1099-NEC reporting those payments — and to file it you need their taxpayer information, collected with Form W-9. (Federal threshold; some states still use $600.)

How W9Track flags it: thresholds follow the selected tax year — $600 for 2025 and earlier, $2,000 for 2026, inflation-adjusted from 2027 (One Big Beautiful Bill Act). A contractor with no W-9 on file turns amber at $400 / $1,500 (“approaching the threshold — collect the W-9 now”) and red at the threshold (“needs 1099 — collect W-9 immediately”). Corporation contractors are generally exempt from 1099 reporting (exceptions include attorneys and medical payments) and are shown with an exempt note.

Not tax advice. Rules have exceptions and change. Confirm with your accountant or the IRS before filing anything.
