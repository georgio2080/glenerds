The 1099 threshold rule

In general, if you pay a contractor at or above the federal reporting threshold in a calendar year for services, you must file a Form 1099-NEC reporting those payments — and to file it you need their taxpayer information, collected with Form W-9. (Federal threshold; some states still use $600.)

How W9Track flags it: thresholds follow the selected tax year — $600 for 2025 and earlier, $2,000 for 2026, inflation-adjusted from 2027 (One Big Beautiful Bill Act). A contractor with no W-9 on file turns amber at $400 / $1,500 (“approaching the threshold — collect the W-9 now”) and red at the threshold (“needs 1099 — collect W-9 immediately”). Corporation contractors are generally exempt from 1099 reporting (exceptions include attorneys and medical payments) and are shown with an exempt note.

Not tax advice. Rules have exceptions and change. Confirm with your accountant or the IRS before filing anything.

Data

Import merges a W9Track JSON file into your data: new contractors are added, new payments are added to matching contractors, and exact duplicates are skipped — re-importing the same file changes nothing. Everything is validated and sanitized: text is length-capped, amounts must be finite numbers ≥ 0, dates must be valid YYYY-MM-DD, and years must be 2000–2099. Bad records are skipped and reported.

Privacy & security

W9Track never stores SSN or EIN numbers — by design there is no field for them. It records only names, contact info, payments, and whether a W-9 is on file (plus the date received).

This page makes zero network requests: no analytics, no tracking pixels, no uploads. It works fully offline, even from a saved copy of this file. Don’t take our word for it — open View Source or your browser’s Network tab and check.

Your data lives only in this browser’s local storage. Clearing browser data erases it, so use Export JSON in the Data section to keep a backup. Keep the signed W-9 forms themselves in your own files — W9Track is the checklist, not the filing cabinet.
