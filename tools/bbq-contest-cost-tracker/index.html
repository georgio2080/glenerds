How to track a contest weekend

Log every dollar the weekend actually consumed: the entry fee, meat, fuel to get there, lodging, and the rubs, injections, and disposables nobody remembers to count. Then log what came back in payouts. The net column is the truth serum — most teams are surprised by their first honest tally, in one direction or the other.

What the numbers are for

This is not about sucking the fun out of competing. It is about deciding which contests deserve next year’s calendar: the $400 weekend an hour away that paid $600 beats the prestige event that cost $1,800 and paid a ribbon. Run a full season through the tracker and the “worth it” ranking writes itself. Teams that split costs get a bonus use — clean, timestamped numbers end every settlement argument.

Worked example

Entry $350, meat $280, fuel $90, lodging $160, supplies $70 — $950 spent. A $1,200 payout leaves a net of +$250. Log a second contest with no payout and the season math turns honest fast — which is the point.

Frequently asked questions

What should I count as a cost?
Everything the contest caused you to spend: entry, meat, fuel, lodging, ice, rubs, disposables, and a share of equipment wear if you want to be thorough. If you would not have spent it without the contest, it counts.
Do I count my time?
For “was it worth it” decisions, yes — at least note the days. Most teams track cash here and treat time as the known cost of the hobby.
What about practice cooks?
Log them as their own entries or fold them into the contest they prepared for. Either way, practice meat is real money.
Can I compare contests?
The table keeps every contest with its net, so best and worst are visible at a glance. Sort mentally by the net column when planning next season.
