How to calculate your net worth

Net worth is simply everything you own minus everything you owe. List bank balances, investment accounts, your home's estimated value, vehicles, and anything else of meaningful value under assets. List credit card balances, student loans, auto loans, mortgages, and any other debts under debts. The tracker does the subtraction and shows the result live as you add items. Most people are surprised the first time — in either direction — which is exactly why the number is worth knowing: you cannot improve what you do not measure.

What to include (and what to skip)

Include anything with real monetary value: cash, savings, brokerage and retirement accounts, home equity (market value minus mortgage), cars at realistic resale value. Skip small household items; tracking every coffee mug adds noise without insight. Update the numbers monthly or quarterly — more often than that and you are mostly watching market noise. The trend over six to twelve months is what matters: is the number climbing, flat, or sliding? A climbing number means your financial system is working, whatever the starting point.

Why track it offline

Net worth is the single most sensitive number in personal finance, and account-aggregator apps ask you to hand it — along with your bank logins — to a third party. This tracker inverts that: the math happens on your device, the data lives in your browser, and there is no company on the other end. You give up automatic syncing; you keep complete privacy. For a number you check a few times a year, that is a trade worth making.

Worked example

Assets: $8,000 in savings and a $12,000 car — $20,000 total. Debts: a $3,500 credit card balance and an $18,000 student loan — $21,500 total. Net worth: −$1,500, shown in red. It stings, but now it's a number instead of a feeling: pay $1,500 off the card and the tracker crosses into positive territory the moment you update it.

Frequently asked questions

Should my home count in net worth?
Yes, at a realistic market value, minus the remaining mortgage. Just remember that home equity is illiquid — it counts, but you cannot spend it without selling or borrowing.
What about my car?
Include it at private-sale value, not what you paid. Cars depreciate, so revisit the number yearly rather than letting an old figure flatter the total.
Is a negative net worth bad?
It is common, especially early in a career with student loans. What matters is the direction: steady upward movement means the plan is working.
Where does my data go?
Nowhere. It is stored in your browser's local storage on your device. Export a JSON backup before clearing browser data or switching devices.
