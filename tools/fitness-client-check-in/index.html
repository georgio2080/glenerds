How to run a weekly check-in

Once a week, log the client’s weight, waist measurement, adherence score, and a few notes on training and nutrition. The table keeps every week in order and shows the weight change from the previous check-in, so trends are visible without digging through old messages. One honest data point per week beats a perfect spreadsheet you never open — consistency of logging matters more than precision of any single number.

What to actually look at

Ignore any single week’s weight; watch the 3–4 week trend. Weight flat while waist shrinks usually means recomposition is working. Adherence scores below 7 two weeks running mean the plan — not the client — needs adjusting. Notes are where the coaching happens: sleep, stress, and travel explain more variance than most coaches admit. Keep the notes short and factual and they become a goldmine at month three.

Worked example

Log Maya's check-ins: week 1 at 172 lb with 7/10 adherence, week 4 at 168 lb with 9/10. The side-by-side comparison shows the trend in seconds — weight down 4 lb as adherence climbed — with no digging through four weeks of chat history to find the numbers.
