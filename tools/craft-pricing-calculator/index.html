How handmade pricing actually works

Most underpricing is not generosity — it is arithmetic that never happened. Add materials, your labor at a real hourly rate (not minimum wage — what your time is worth), a slice of overhead, then divide by what is left after fees and your target margin. The formula above does exactly that: price = true cost ÷ (1 − fee% − margin%). Enter your current price too and you get an honest Healthy / Thin / Underwater verdict on it.

The labor trap

Labor is where handmade pricing dies. “It only took me an hour” at a made-up $10/hour is how a $4 candle sells for $8 and nets $2. Time yourself across a real batch, use an hourly rate you would accept from an employer, and put that number in without flinching. If the resulting price feels too high for your market, the answer is rarely to cut your rate — it is to simplify the product, raise perceived value, or accept that this particular item is not a viable product.

Worked example

If you're currently charging $45, the verdict reads Underwater at −28.7% margin — the fees and labor alone eat the price. That's the number most sellers are missing.

Frequently asked questions

What margin should I target?
Thirty percent profit after fees is a healthy baseline for handmade goods; wholesale needs room for the retailer’s markup on top, so price wholesale at roughly half of retail and check the math still works.
Should I count my own labor?
Yes — always. Unpaid labor is a hobby subsidy, not a business model. If the price only works with free labor, the product does not work.
What counts as overhead?
Anything the product consumes that is not direct materials: packaging, labels, a share of tools, electricity, market booth fees spread across units. A dollar or two per item is a sane starting point.
How do different sales channels change the price?
Fees differ — your own site, a marketplace, a craft fair, wholesale — so the same item needs a different price per channel. Re-run the calculator with each channel’s fee to keep every channel profitable.
What if I sell many different products?
